- Wikipedia, 'Eskild Andersen Kongsbakke' (c.1620 Kungsbacka; commoner; joined the company 1639, sailed on the Christianshavn, arrived 1643; quartermaster under Leyel; chosen by soldiers and inhabitants after Korsør's death in 1655 because no Danish officers remained, almost illiterate; 1655 siege withstood with local support; a nine-month siege; town wall credited to him by Sethuraman, disputed by Bjørn Westerbeek Dahl; privateering against Mughal Bengal, trade with Bantam and Makassar; Denmark sceptical of his reports; Geert van Hagen sent 1668; Færø reached Tranquebar May 1669 with Henrik Eggers, who was to take over if Kongsbakke had died; villages of Poreiar, Tillali and Erikutanchery ceded; royal letter of 10 Dec 1670 reprimanded both men but reconfirmed Kongsbakke; quote 'That is the honor one gets for 5 years of service in Europe and 30 years in India'; Eggers sent to Copenhagen 1672 to explain himself; died at Dansborg c.1674; presumed Indian wife; Kay Larsen 1907 'the brave and loyal man who saved the fortress'; recent research less heroic, multicultural enclave)
- Wikipedia, 'Sieges of Tranquebar (1655-1669)' (Vijaya Raghava needed money for campaigns against Gingee and Madurai; higher tribute demanded 1655; Christianshavn the last Danish ship for 26 years; 1648 mutiny, Leyel arrested; company dissolved 1650; Korsør died 7 September 1655; 1655 defence with local Indian and Portuguese mercenaries; 1660 siege of nine months; wall around the whole town and four new towers; before that only Dansborg fortified; peace in 1669)
- Wikipedia, 'Danish India' (treaty with the Nayak of Tanjore, 20 November 1620, village granted for tribute; Tranquebar sold to Britain in 1845)
